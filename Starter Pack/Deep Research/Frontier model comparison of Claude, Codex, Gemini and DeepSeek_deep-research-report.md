# Frontier model comparison of Claude, Codex, Gemini and DeepSeek

## TLDR

If you weight **hard-to-game, contamination-resistant, real-work benchmarks** more heavily than headline-friendly legacy numbers, the frontier does **not** collapse into one clean winner. The current picture is: **Claude Opus 4.8** has the strongest claim to the overall lead in independent composite intelligence and agentic knowledge-work performance; **GPT-5.5** remains exceptionally strong for execution-heavy coding, computer use, and operational workflows; **Gemini 3.1 Deep Think / Gemini 3.1 Pro** is the strongest choice for academic-scientific reasoning, multimodal reasoning, and long-context synthesis; and **DeepSeek V4 Pro** is the strongest **open-weight / value** option, especially for coding, but still trails the top closed models overall. citeturn15view0turn13view1turn18view0turn34search1

Your two hearsay claims resolve unevenly. The claim that **Gemini is the best at UI design** is **not supported by the strongest current public leaderboard evidence**: the public WebDev Arena is currently led by Anthropic models, with GPT-5.5 ahead of Gemini 3.1 Pro there as well. Anthropic’s current docs show Opus 4.8 uses **adaptive thinking** rather than manual token budgets, and supports **low, medium, high, xhigh, and max**, with **high** as the default. citeturn6view4turn26search13turn6view0

## Benchmark weighting

The most important distinction in this comparison is between **popular benchmarks that people quote because the numbers are big and familiar**, and **benchmarks that are specifically designed to resist contamination, memorisation, or easy benchmark-chasing**. I would not discard the popular ones completely, because buyers, founders, and social-media discourse still react to them. But I would **weight them less** whenever there is evidence that a dataset is saturated, static, or memorisable. citeturn29search1turn29search2turn35view0turn30search1

The biggest current warning light is software-engineering benchmarking. **SWE-Bench Verified** and even some newer public SWE-bench-style numbers still matter, but they should be read with caution. Anthropic explicitly says its memorisation screens flag a subset of **SWE-Bench Verified, Pro, and Multilingual** problems, and Scale’s **SWE-Bench Pro** was built specifically because older public SWE-bench variants could not reliably separate true problem-solving from recall. That is exactly the kind of “100% because it trained on the test” failure mode you said you wanted called out. citeturn30search1turn35view0

A second warning light is **benchmark saturation**. MMLU is the classic example: still wildly quoted, but no longer the right place to decide between frontier models. MMLU-Pro is much better than MMLU, but even MMLU-Pro should be treated as **one input**, not the decision-maker. By contrast, HLE, GPQA, LiveCodeBench, and agentic environment benchmarks still have meaningful headroom and are much harder to bluff. citeturn29search1turn29search2turn29search3turn29search0

## Peak-setting results

At **peak effort**, the cleanest independent overall read comes from **Artificial Analysis**. On its current Intelligence Index, **Claude Opus 4.8 (max)** leads with **61.4**, **GPT-5.5 (xhigh)** follows at **60**, **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** is at **57**, and **DeepSeek V4 Pro (max)** is at **52**. Artificial Analysis also states a **95% confidence interval of ±1 point** on this index, so Opus 4.8’s lead over GPT-5.5 is real but **narrow**, while anything clustered near 57 should be treated as effectively tied unless another benchmark clearly breaks the tie. citeturn15view0turn13view0turn13view1turn24search0turn34search1turn21search4

On **real-world knowledge-work agency**, Anthropic currently has the best overall evidence. Artificial Analysis says **Opus 4.8 scored 1890 Elo on GDPval-AA**, implying roughly a **67% win rate against GPT-5.5**, and notes meaningful gains in terminal use and scientific reasoning as well. Anthropic’s own launch materials also include partner evidence that **Opus 4.8 scored 84% on Online-Mind2Web** and was the strongest browser/computer-use model that partner had tested. That combination is why I would currently route **deep research, multi-step planning, structured analysis, and “produce the full deliverable” work** to Claude first. citeturn15view0turn36view2turn36view3

OpenAI’s strongest case is still **execution-heavy operational work**. GPT-5.5’s official page reports **84.9% on GDPval**, **78.7% on OSWorld-Verified**, **98.0% on τ²-Bench Telecom**, and strong performance on FinanceAgent and OfficeQA Pro. OpenAI also says the headline GPT benchmark runs on its launch page were executed with **reasoning effort set to xhigh**. In practice, I read that bundle as: if the task is “use the computer, deal with tools, finish the workflow, don’t get lost halfway through”, GPT-5.5 is still absolutely top-tier, even if Anthropic has now taken a narrow overall lead on some independent agentic composites. citeturn8search0turn9view1turn7search1

Google’s best case is the clearest in **frontier academic and multimodal reasoning**. On Google’s own Deep Think page, **Gemini 3.1 Deep Think** posts **48.4% on HLE without tools**, **53.4% on HLE with search+code**, **84.6% on ARC-AGI-2**, **81.5% on MMMU-Pro**, **81.5% on IMO 2025**, and **3455 Codeforces Elo**. Even if one discounts vendor self-reporting somewhat, that is a very strong and very coherent pattern: Gemini is unusually strong when the problem is broad, multimodal, knowledge-heavy, and intellectually “Olympiad-like”. citeturn18view0

DeepSeek V4 Pro’s peak setting is the easiest to characterise: it is **not** the overall leader, but it is the most compelling **open-weight bargain** in this set. Its official table gives **93.5 LiveCodeBench**, **67.9 Terminal-Bench**, **55.4 SWE-Bench Pro**, **37.7 HLE**, **87.5 MMLU-Pro**, and **90.1 GPQA** at **Max** effort; Artificial Analysis separately scores it at **52** on the broader Intelligence Index. That is a strong result for an MIT-licensed, far cheaper model, but it still sits meaningfully below the very top proprietary models on broad general intelligence and multimodal capability. citeturn6view2turn34search1

## Medium-setting results

This is where the public evidence becomes much weaker and much messier. **OpenAI is the only one of the four with a clean, public, independent “medium” benchmark point that is easy to use**. GPT-5.5 defaults to **medium** reasoning effort, and Artificial Analysis reports **GPT-5.5 (medium) = 57** on its Intelligence Index. Artificial Analysis also says that this matches **Claude Opus 4.7 (max)** on the same index while costing much less to evaluate. If you want a single “balanced” lower-latency frontier setting with solid public evidence behind it, GPT-5.5 medium is the cleanest answer in the market right now. citeturn7search1turn20search1turn15view1turn20search11

For **Anthropic**, the problem is not support but transparency. Anthropic does support **medium** on Opus 4.8, but it has **not** published a broad public medium-effort benchmark matrix the way OpenAI has effectively enabled through third-party testing. The best current medium-effort public number I found is from **Zapier’s AutomationBench**, where **Claude Opus 4.8 Medium scores 11.1%**, compared with **15.5% at XHigh** and **15.4% at Max**. That is useful for real-work execution trade-offs, but it is not enough to generate a full medium-effort apples-to-apples benchmark stack for Claude yet. citeturn26search13turn37view3

For **Google**, the situation is similar but slightly worse. Google officially supports **medium** on Gemini 3.1 Pro, yet its public benchmark tables are published at **Thinking (High)**, which is also the model’s default. The public **57** score for Gemini 3.1 Pro on Artificial Analysis should therefore be read as a **default/high** score, **not** as a true medium number. In other words: medium exists, but public medium benchmarking is not currently transparent enough to compare honestly with OpenAI medium. citeturn18view1turn18view2turn24search0

For **DeepSeek**, a “medium” row would be misleading because the company’s public V4 Pro benchmark table does **not** publish one. The closest lower-compute public point is **High**, where DeepSeek V4 Pro scores **50** on Artificial Analysis’ Intelligence Index and officially reports **89.8 LiveCodeBench**, **63.3 Terminal-Bench**, **54.4 SWE-Bench Pro**, and **34.5 HLE**. That lower point is still very competitive for the money, but it is not a literal medium tier. citeturn34search3turn6view2

The consequence is straightforward: if you insist on a strict **“max versus medium”** scoreboard, the public evidence is currently **incomplete** for Anthropic and Google, and only **partially mappable** for DeepSeek. That is not a failure of analysis; it is a failure of current public reporting by the model vendors and benchmarking ecosystem. citeturn26search13turn18view1turn6view2

## Open questions and limitations

The biggest limitation is **medium-effort transparency**. **OpenAI** publishes and enables the clearest public “medium” benchmark story. **Anthropic** supports medium on Opus 4.8, but broad current medium-effort benchmark reporting is sparse. **Google** supports medium on Gemini 3.1 Pro, but its public benchmark pages are published at **high/default** or **Deep Think**, not medium. **DeepSeek** does not publish a medium tier for V4 Pro at all. That means any strict “all four at medium” table has to include **missing values or nearest honest proxies**. citeturn7search1turn26search13turn18view1turn6view2

A second limitation is that many of the vendor benchmark tables are still **self-reported**, and the harnesses are not always identical. Where possible, I have leaned harder on **independent sources** such as Artificial Analysis, Scale’s **SWE-Bench Pro**, Zapier’s **AutomationBench**, and the public **WebDev Arena**. Where only vendor numbers existed, I still reported them, but I weighted them less if the benchmark was static or especially easy to optimise for. citeturn13view3turn35view0turn28view0turn33view0

A third limitation is that **Google’s “max-equivalent”** is a little conceptually different from Anthropic / OpenAI / DeepSeek effort settings. **Deep Think** is the right practical mapping for your “best of the best” bucket, but it is not a simple one-click equivalent of “xhigh” or “max” on every benchmark page. I used it anyway because it is the closest honest analogue to the question you asked. citeturn18view0turn18view1
